About

I am a PhD candidate in economics at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, advised by Dr. Trey Miller, and a research assistant at the Boyd Center for Business and Economic Research. My research is in the economics of education and labor economics. I study how state and local policies shape educational and economic mobility, with current work on automatic enrollment in advanced courses, teacher incentive pay, and kindergarten retention in Texas.

Before graduate school, I taught Algebra I and Pre-Algebra and worked as an instructional coach in Dallas ISD from 2018 to 2023.

Research

Working Papers

Closing the Gaps: An Examination of Early Impacts of Dallas ISD's Opt-Out Policy on Advanced Course Enrollment

with D. Vargas-Castaño and T. Miller

Under review at Education Finance and Policy · EdWorkingPapers No. 25-1184

Abstract

Advanced high school courses predict subsequent student success, but fewer Black and Hispanic students take advanced courses compared to their White peers. One strategy to increase advanced course enrollment is to use an “opt-out” approach, in which all students are enrolled in advanced courses unless they decline. We use a synthetic control design to evaluate the impact of an opt-out policy in Dallas Independent School District (DISD), a large urban district in Texas, on the early uptake of Algebra I in the 2019-20 school year. The policy increased enrollment and completion of Algebra I before high school by 11.7 and 10.5 percentage points respectively relative to a synthetic district. The Hispanic–White enrollment gap decreased substantially, while there was a small but insignificant increase in the Black–White enrollment gap. Descriptive analysis suggests that Black students did not benefit as much, likely due in part to having fewer students meet the auto enrollment criterion, more students leaving DISD, and fewer non-honors track students taking Algebra I by 8th grade.

Media coverage: Education Week; Fordham Institute; EdWorkingPapers Policy and Practice Series
